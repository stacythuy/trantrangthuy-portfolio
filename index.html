My Story

Growing up twice: Moving between cultures

Growing up in Vietnam and moving to Singapore in Secondary 3 exposed me to people who approached the same questions, challenges, and experiences in very different ways. That awareness made me more attentive to perspectives beyond my own, and it has shaped the way I approach learning, leadership, and people.

In Singapore, I did not begin with a familiar network or environment. I started by gradually contributing and suggesting initiatives: at debate training, CCA meetings, and community activities like nursing home visits and student association events. Over time, that consistency grew into responsibility, and responsibility became leadership. These experiences continue to shape how I build student-led initiatives that connect ideas, people, and action — grounded in underserved needs and social impact.

Reflections

Moments that Shaped Me

01

Leading through uncertainty: Founding the ADC

I used to think leadership meant having all the answers. Planning the ASEAN Debating Championship changed that. When schedules shifted, participants withdrew, and the tabulation system failed, I could not solve every problem alone. Instead, I focused on keeping the team calm, redistributing tasks, and adapting under pressure. Through this experience, I learned that leadership is about enabling the team to move forward despite uncertainty.

02

Creating the path: The courage to start

Starting Learn2earn proves that opportunities rarely arrive fully formed. By introducing youth to entrepreneurship through fundraising workshops, I saw how thoughtful ideas can develop into meaningful impact. Through the experience, I realised that opportunities are often created rather than found. Positive change begins when someone takes initiative, turns ideas into action, and creates opportunities for others.

03

The art of presence in service

In the beginning, I prepared structured activities for each visit — games, songs, and discussion topics. One afternoon, Ms Tan simply wanted to talk about her late husband. No activity, no structure, just listening. I realised then that the best form of service is not always what you prepare, but how fully you are willing to listen. That afternoon reshaped how I view community work. What matters is not what you bring, but your presence.

04

Beyond the need for answers

I entered the project expecting to find answers; I left with better questions. Unexpected results in our piezoelectricity research often challenged our predictions, forcing me to reconsider underlying assumptions and independently explore concepts beyond the classroom. Through repeated refinement of our approach, I learned that intellectual curiosity is not about reaching certainty, but about thinking more deeply when answers contradict.

Founder & Organiser 2025-2026

I initiated the inaugural ASEAN Debating Championship (ADC), a student-led regional debate tournament bringing together participants from 5 ASEAN countries and 2 local schools. From conception to execution, I led the tournament's development, driving international outreach and participant engagement while overseeing logistics, tabulation, and operations to create a platform for cross-border dialogue, competition, and collaboration.

Key Contributions

ADC created a tangible pathway for debate teams across the region to compete and connect. Participants cited it as their first experience of international-level debate in a student-organised and ASEAN-centered format.

Running ADC taught me that leading is less about having all the answers and more about holding the team together and pioneering the way when the answers are unclear. I learned I am energised by complexity, not paralysed by it.

Blue Ocean Competition 2025

Recycled Cooking Oil Cleaning Product

Concerned by the environmental impact of improperly disposed cooking oil, my team developed a circular-economy business concept that converts waste cooking oil into eco-friendly household cleaning products. Through market research, business modelling, and sustainability analysis, we explored how entrepreneurship can transform environmental challenges into practical and scalable solutions.

Key Contributions
  • Ranked among the Top 350 teams globally (top 2%) from 12,000+ participants across 163 countries
  • Researched consumer demand, pricing, and sustainable cleaning products
  • Developed business and sustainability models for recycled cooking oil products
  • Assessed product feasibility through competitor and industry analysis
  • Pitched venture concept to competition judges

The project showed me that sustainability is most effective when environmental impact and economic value align. Lasting solutions are often those that make doing the right thing both practical and worthwhile.

Bronze Awardee 2025

Piezoelectric-Powered MRT Tapping Machines - SISTEMIC

Interested in how technology can be applied to sustainability challenges in urban environments, my team developed a prototype exploring how footstep energy in MRT stations could be harvested through piezoelectric sensors to power tapping machines. The project combined engineering design, renewable energy concepts, and practical problem-solving to explore innovative approaches to reducing energy consumption in public infrastructure.

Key Contributions
  • Awarded Bronze at the Singapore International STEM Innovation Challenge (SISTEMIC), organised by NJC and MOE
  • Built a prototype that converted footstep pressure into electrical energy
  • Modelled projected energy output using commuter footfall data
  • Researched piezoelectric energy harvesting and urban sustainability applications
  • Co-authored technical reports and presented findings to judges

The project taught me that innovative ideas are only as valuable as their feasibility. Turning a concept into a workable solution required balancing creativity with technical constraints, data, and real-world practicality.

Believing that sustainability is most effective when it resonates with people's identities and values, I co-developed a circular fashion business concept that transforms textile factory offcuts into Qipao-inspired pouches and Chinese-knot keychains. The project explored how cultural heritage, product design, and entrepreneurship can work together to reduce textile waste while creating products with social and commercial value.

Key Contributions

  • Designed Qipao-inspired accessories from surplus textile materials
  • Developed a circular production model from sourcing to distribution
  • Assessed market demand, pricing, and product viability
  • Developed a business proposal for a national-level trade competition

The project showed me that sustainability is most powerful when it connects with culture and identity.

Sterilised Pneumatic Tube Transport System

Inspired by the challenges of accessing essential goods and services during the COVID-19 pandemic, I conducted an independent research project exploring the use of sterilised pneumatic tube systems. The project examined how automated transport networks could improve the delivery of supplies and daily necessities while enhancing operational efficiency and accessibility.

Key Contributions
  • Awarded Third Prize in a school-level research competition
  • Advanced as a finalist to the city-level research competition
  • Evaluated sterilisation methods for enclosed transport networks
  • Developed an engineering proposal for dormitory and elderly care settings
  • Produced and presented a formal research report

As my first research project, it sparked my interest in designing practical solutions to everyday challenges — an interest that continues to shape many of my projects today.

HCMC Scientific Research Competition Third Prize · 2023

Behavioural Research on Time Management of High School Students

I conducted a behavioural research study examining time management patterns among high school students, their relationship to academic performance and wellbeing, and the interventions most effective at improving planning behaviour. The study was submitted to the Ho Chi Minh City City-wide Scientific Research Competition, where it received a Third Prize.

Key Contributions
  • Designed research methodology including survey instruments and data collection
  • Collected and analysed data from a sample of high school student respondents
  • Identified key patterns in procrastination, goal-setting and planning behaviour
  • Produced a written research paper and presented findings to competition judges
  • Awarded Third Prize at city-wide competition level

Researching time management as a student who was trying to manage my own time was both ironic and instructive. Understanding the behavioural science behind something you live every day changes how you approach it.
