My Story

A Student Shaped by Two Worlds

Growing up in Vietnam and moving to Singapore for secondary school gave me something no textbook could — an instinctive sense of how different people see the same world differently. It made me genuinely curious about other perspectives, and that curiosity has driven everything I do.

In Singapore, I did not have an existing network or a familiar language. What I had was the willingness to show up — at debate training, at CCA meetings, at nursing home visits. Over time, showing up became leading, and leading became something I grew to love not for the title, but for what it enabled in others.

My leadership philosophy is simple: the best leaders create more leaders. Whether I am organising a regional competition or sitting beside an elderly resident who wants someone to talk to, I try to act from that same place of genuine attention and care.

Reflections

Moments That Shaped Me

01

What Organising the ADC Taught Me About Leadership

I used to think leadership meant having all the answers. Planning the ASEAN Debating Championship changed that. There were moments when nothing went to plan — venues fell through, speakers cancelled, coordinating across three time zones proved harder than expected. What carried us through was not my ability to solve every problem alone, but my ability to keep the team calm, redistribute tasks, and remind everyone why we had started. I learned that leadership is less about vision and more about presence.

02

Growing Up Twice: Moving Between Cultures

Being Vietnamese in Singapore made me feel like I was always slightly translating — not just language, but values, humour, expectations. At first this was uncomfortable. Now I see it as a gift. I move between perspectives more easily than most, and in debate and MUN, that has been my quiet advantage. When I chair a committee or argue a position, I am genuinely aware of how it lands on different ears. Cross-cultural fluency is not something you can teach in a classroom. You earn it through discomfort.

03

The Nursing Home and the Art of Slow Presence

In the beginning, I prepared activities for each visit — games, songs, topics to discuss. One afternoon, Mr Tan just wanted to talk about his late wife. No activity. No structure. Just listening. I realised then that the best service is not always a programme you deliver; sometimes it is simply a willingness to be still and be present. That afternoon reset how I think about community work. The value is not in what you bring. It is in how fully you arrive.

04

Balancing the Ledger: Academics and Leadership

There were terms when I was simultaneously preparing for examinations, leading CCA, coordinating ADC logistics and attending service visits. I will be honest — some weeks I did not balance anything. I simply chose what mattered most that day and trusted I could catch up on the rest. What I discovered was that these experiences made me better at academics, not worse. The ability to think on my feet, to write persuasively, to distil a complex issue — all of it came from doing, not just studying.

CCA Vice President 2025–2026

Debate & Oratorical CCA — Vice President

As Vice President of the school's Debate & Oratorical CCA, I was responsible for the day-to-day running of training sessions, mentorship of junior members, and building the culture of the team. The role required balancing people management, curriculum design and external competition coordination across two academic years.

Key Contributions
  • Planned and led weekly training sessions for 40+ members
  • Developed motion bank and case study resources for juniors
  • Pair-mentored 6 Sec 1 and Sec 2 debaters through their first competitions
  • Represented CCA in school leadership council meetings
  • Coordinated logistics for interschool tournaments and invitationals

The most rewarding part of being VP was not winning competitions — it was watching a nervous Sec 1 student deliver a confident rebuttal by the end of the year. That is the work that lasts.

Founder & Organiser 2025-2026

ASEAN Debating Championship (ADC)

The inaugural ASEAN Debating Championship is an inter-school debate competition I initiated to bridge the gap between national debate programmes across Southeast Asia. The two-day tournament brought together student debaters from 5 ASEAN countries, and beyond for competitive rounds, feedback sessions and cross-cultural debate — entirely student-organised.

Key Contributions
  • Conceptualised and pitched the event to school leaders from scratch
  • Coordinated with participating schools and teams across 5 countries
  • Recruited, briefed and managed a panel of experienced adjudicators
  • Handled tabulation, scheduling and all cross-team communications
  • Facilitated cross-school feedback and networking sessions post-rounds
Impact

ADC created a tangible pathway for debate teams across the region to compete and connect. Participants cited it as their first experience of international-level debate in a student-organised and ASEAN-centered format.

Running ADC taught me that leading is less about having all the answers and more about holding the team together and pioneering the way when the answers are unclear. I learned I am energised by complexity, not paralysed by it.

Founder & President 2024

Learn2Earn — Entrepreneurship CCA

I founded Learn2Earn as a student-run entrepreneurship CCA to give peers practical exposure to business thinking, financial literacy and real-world problem-solving. From writing the CCA proposal to recruiting members and designing its first programme calendar, I built the CCA from the ground up while managing its day-to-day operations as President.

Key Contributions
  • Wrote and submitted the official CCA proposal to school leadership
  • Recruited founding member cohort and structured programme curriculum
  • Organised workshops on entrepreneurship, pitching and financial literacy
  • Led the CCA's volunteer day initiative at an HCMC orphanage
  • Oversaw CCA's participation in school-level competitions

Founding a CCA from nothing taught me that the hardest part of leadership is not managing people — it is managing ambiguity. When there is no template, you become the template.

Lead Organiser 2023–2024

Vietnamese Youth Alliance in Singapore (VYNAS)

As Lead Organiser for VYNAS, I planned and executed three large-scale keystone events for the Vietnamese student community in Singapore — the Study Abroad Dream forum, a Career Fair, and a cultural Concert. Each event required extensive coordination across sponsors, vendors, speakers and volunteers, while serving a community that often lacks access to structured support networks.

Key Contributions
  • Led end-to-end event planning for three flagship VYNAS events
  • Managed sponsor outreach, logistics and volunteer coordination
  • Curated speaker lineups for Study Abroad Dream and Career Fair
  • Oversaw production and programming for the annual Concert
  • Built community ties between Vietnamese students across Singapore schools
Impact

Each event drew strong attendance and positive feedback. The Career Fair and Study Abroad forum gave Vietnamese students practical access to guidance and networks they often cannot find elsewhere.

Organising events for my own community felt different from everything else I had done. I was not just executing a programme — I was building something I genuinely wished had existed when I first arrived in Singapore, alone and uncertain. Every Vietnamese student who walked into the Career Fair or the Study Abroad Dream forum was someone I recognised: someone trying to figure out how to belong in two places at once. Leading VYNAS taught me that the most powerful form of service is not giving people resources — it is giving them a room where they feel understood. When the Concert audience sang together, when a student told me the Career Fair changed how they thought about their future, I understood for the first time what it means to build community rather than just participate in it. This work is not finished. It is the kind of work that outlasts any single event.

Blue Ocean Competition 2025

Recycled Cooking Oil Cleaning Product

Inspired by the circular economy, I developed a concept for converting waste cooking oil into a biodegradable cleaning product — presented at the Blue Ocean Student Entrepreneur Competition. The idea addresses two parallel problems: the improper disposal of used cooking oil and the growing demand for non-toxic, eco-friendly household cleaners.

Key Contributions
  • Researched saponification chemistry and formulation feasibility
  • Built a business model canvas with cost, pricing and sustainability analysis
  • Benchmarked against existing products (Finish Dishwasher Tablets, Sunlight Floor Cleaner)
  • Prototyped basic product formulation with teacher guidance
  • Pitched to competition panel — noted for commercial and environmental feasibility
Impact

The project demonstrated that sustainability and commercial viability are not opposites — a practical circular solution can also be a viable business.

This project reminded me: you do not need to solve climate change to do something meaningful. Find one problem that is small enough to act on — and act on it well.

SISTEMIC @NJC STEM Competition

Generating AC Current for MRT Tapping Machines via Piezo Sensors

For the SISTEMIC international STEM competition at NJC, I worked on a project exploring how footstep energy from commuters at MRT tapping machines could be harvested using piezoelectric sensors to generate AC current — making the machines partially or fully self-powered. The project combined electrical engineering principles with urban sustainability design.

Key Contributions
  • Researched piezoelectric energy harvesting principles and applications
  • Modelled energy output estimates based on commuter footfall data
  • Designed a conceptual integration system for MRT tapping machine architecture
  • Presented project findings and prototype concept to international competition judges
Impact

The project proposed a scalable, low-disruption approach to embedding renewable energy generation into existing urban infrastructure — relevant to Singapore's Smart Nation and sustainability agenda.

Working on this project taught me that sustainability solutions do not always require new infrastructure — sometimes the most elegant answer is embedded in what already exists.

Upcycling Initiative 2023–2024

Textile Waste Upcycling

I designed a circular fashion concept centred on upcycling textile factory offcuts into Qipao-inspired pouches and Chinese-knot keychains. The project explored how mass production systems and digital fabrication technology could scale artisanal upcycling into a commercially viable and culturally meaningful product line.

Key Contributions
  • Sourced textile waste samples and researched available factory surplus streams
  • Designed product prototypes inspired by traditional Chinese aesthetics
  • Mapped a circular production model from waste input to finished product
  • Assessed commercial viability and pricing for target consumer segments

Fashion is one of the world's most polluting industries — and one of the least discussed in sustainability circles. This project showed me that culture and sustainability can reinforce each other rather than compete.

Research Project 2023

Sterilised Vacuum Tube Systems for Hospital Supply Transport

Inspired by challenges exposed during the COVID-19 pandemic, I researched the feasibility of building sterilised vacuum tube systems to transport medical supplies within hospitals and quarantine facilities — reducing physical contact between staff and potentially contaminated materials during outbreak scenarios.

Key Contributions
  • Reviewed existing pneumatic tube system technology in hospital settings
  • Researched sterilisation methods compatible with enclosed transport systems
  • Assessed feasibility and cost-effectiveness for pandemic-use quarantine facilities
  • Produced a written research report with design recommendations
Impact

The research highlighted a practical infrastructure gap in pandemic preparedness — one that existing technology could plausibly address with targeted adaptation.

The pandemic made visible problems that had always existed in healthcare logistics. This project taught me to look at crises not just as tragedies, but as invitations to solve something that was always overdue.

HCMC Scientific Research Competition Third Prize · 2023

Behavioural Research on Time Management of High School Students

I conducted a behavioural research study examining time management patterns among high school students, their relationship to academic performance and wellbeing, and the interventions most effective at improving planning behaviour. The study was submitted to the Ho Chi Minh City City-wide Scientific Research Competition, where it received a Third Prize.

Key Contributions
  • Designed research methodology including survey instruments and data collection
  • Collected and analysed data from a sample of high school student respondents
  • Identified key patterns in procrastination, goal-setting and planning behaviour
  • Produced a written research paper and presented findings to competition judges
  • Awarded Third Prize at city-wide competition level

Researching time management as a student who was trying to manage my own time was both ironic and instructive. Understanding the behavioural science behind something you live every day changes how you approach it.
