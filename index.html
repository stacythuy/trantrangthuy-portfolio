My Story

A Student Shaped by Two Worlds

Growing up in Vietnam and moving to Singapore for secondary school gave me something no textbook could — an instinctive sense of how different people see the same world differently. It made me genuinely curious about other perspectives, and that curiosity has driven everything I do.

In Singapore, I did not have an existing network or a familiar language. What I had was the willingness to show up — at debate training, at CCA meetings, at nursing home visits. Over time, showing up became leading, and leading became something I grew to love not for the title, but for what it enabled in others.

My leadership philosophy is simple: the best leaders create more leaders. Whether I am organising a regional competition or sitting beside an elderly resident who wants someone to talk to, I try to act from that same place of genuine attention and care.

Reflections

Moments That Shaped Me

01

What Organising the ADC Taught Me About Leadership

I used to think leadership meant having all the answers. Planning the ASEAN Debating Championship changed that. There were moments when nothing went to plan — venues fell through, speakers cancelled, coordinating across three time zones proved harder than expected. What carried us through was not my ability to solve every problem alone, but my ability to keep the team calm, redistribute tasks, and remind everyone why we had started. I learned that leadership is less about vision and more about presence.

02

Growing Up Twice: Moving Between Cultures

Being Vietnamese in Singapore made me feel like I was always slightly translating — not just language, but values, humour, expectations. At first this was uncomfortable. Now I see it as a gift. I move between perspectives more easily than most, and in debate and MUN, that has been my quiet advantage. When I chair a committee or argue a position, I am genuinely aware of how it lands on different ears. Cross-cultural fluency is not something you can teach in a classroom. You earn it through discomfort.

03

The Nursing Home and the Art of Slow Presence

In the beginning, I prepared activities for each visit — games, songs, topics to discuss. One afternoon, Mr Tan just wanted to talk about his late wife. No activity. No structure. Just listening. I realised then that the best service is not always a programme you deliver; sometimes it is simply a willingness to be still and be present. That afternoon reset how I think about community work. The value is not in what you bring. It is in how fully you arrive.

04

Balancing the Ledger: Academics and Leadership

There were terms when I was simultaneously preparing for examinations, leading CCA, coordinating ADC logistics and attending service visits. I will be honest — some weeks I did not balance anything. I simply chose what mattered most that day and trusted I could catch up on the rest. What I discovered was that these experiences made me better at academics, not worse. The ability to think on my feet, to write persuasively, to distil a complex issue — all of it came from doing, not just studying.

05

On Sustainability: Why I Believe in Practical Solutions

A lot of sustainability conversation feels distant — global targets, abstract pledges, enormous problems. What motivates me is the small, specific, do-able thing. Could waste cooking oil become a cleaning product? Could textile offcuts be turned into something useful? These questions feel urgent precisely because they are answerable. I do not want to wait until I am older or better-resourced to engage with environmental challenges. The projects I have worked on in secondary school have convinced me that students can prototype, test and communicate solutions that matter — and that doing so builds habits of mind that will last far beyond any competition.

Founder & Organiser 2024

ASEAN Debating Championship (ADC)

The ASEAN Debating Championship is an inter-school debate competition I founded to bridge the gap between national debate programmes across Southeast Asia. The event brought together student debaters from Singapore, Vietnam, and beyond for two days of competitive debate, feedback sessions and cross-cultural dialogue.

Key Contributions
  • Conceptualised and pitched the event to school leadership
  • Coordinated with participating schools across 3 countries
  • Recruited and briefed a panel of experienced judges
  • Managed speaker logistics, scheduling and communications
  • Facilitated cross-school feedback sessions post-rounds
Impact

ADC created a tangible pathway for debate teams from different countries to compete, learn and build relationships. Participants cited it as their first experience of international-level debate in a student-organised format.

Running ADC taught me that leading is less about having the answers and more about holding the team together when the answers are unclear. The event also revealed something about myself: I am energised by complexity, not paralysed by it.

Programme Lead 2023–2024

Nursing Home Engagement Programme

Over two semesters, I designed and led a weekly engagement programme for elderly residents at a local nursing home. The programme combined structured activities — including storytelling circles, simple games and craft sessions — with unstructured time for genuine conversation.

Key Contributions
  • Designed and ran fortnightly activity sessions for 15 residents
  • Trained and briefed 8 student volunteers each session
  • Coordinated with nursing home staff on resident needs
  • Documented sessions and reported outcomes to service-learning coordinator
  • Continued visiting informally beyond the formal programme
Impact

Several residents mentioned the sessions as highlights of their week. Two residents asked specifically for the same student volunteers — a small thing that meant a great deal.

The most important lesson from this programme: the people we serve often give us more than we give them. Every resident I met left me with something — a story, a perspective, a reminder of what matters.

Business Concept 2024

Recycled Cooking Oil Cleaning Product

Inspired by the circular economy, I developed a concept for converting waste cooking oil into a biodegradable cleaning product. The idea addresses two parallel problems: the improper disposal of cooking oil and the demand for non-toxic household cleaners.

Key Contributions
  • Researched saponification process and soap chemistry
  • Developed business model canvas and sustainability impact analysis
  • Prototyped basic formulation with teacher guidance
  • Presented to panel at school sustainability competition
  • Won Sustainability Innovation Prize at school level
Impact

The project demonstrated that sustainability and commercial viability are not opposites. The panel noted its practical feasibility as a particular strength.

This project reminded me that you do not need to solve climate change to do something meaningful. Sometimes it is about finding one problem that is small enough to act on — and acting on it well.

STEM Research 2023

Sea-Level Rise Environmental Project

A STEM-based research project examining the projected impact of sea-level rise on low-lying coastal communities, with a focus on Singapore and Vietnam. The project combined data analysis with community impact storytelling.

Key Contributions
  • Compiled and visualised sea-level projection data from IPCC reports
  • Mapped impact on specific communities in Vietnam's Mekong Delta
  • Developed policy recommendation brief for school audience
  • Presented findings at school science symposium

Working on this project changed how I see Vietnam — not just as home, but as a place genuinely at risk. That personal stake made the research feel less like an assignment and more like a responsibility.
